Module 1: Mixing Hot and Cold Liquids (Polystyrene Cups)

Hot water is poured into cold water inside insulated polystyrene cups. Because polystyrene is an insulator, heat loss to the surroundings is minimized.

Governing Principle: Conservation of Energy
Energy Lost by Hot Water = Energy Gained by Cold Water
C₁ · (T₁ - Teq) = C₂ · (Teq - T₂) &implies; Teq = (C₁T₁ + C₂T₂) / (C₁ + C₂)

Experimental Parameters

Hot Water Mass (m₁): 0.10 kg
Hot Water Initial Temp (T₁): 80 °C
Cold Water Mass (m₂): 0.15 kg
Cold Water Initial Temp (T₂): 20 °C

Calculation & Real-Time Results

Heat Capacity of Cold Water (C₂ = m₂ · c): 630.0 J °C⁻¹
Heat Capacity of Hot Water (C₁ = m₁ · c): 420.0 J °C⁻¹
Equilibrium Final Temperature (Teq): 44.0 °C

Module 2: Chinese Tea Problem & Energy Block Model

Problem: Kate pours 0.2 kg of boiling water (100 °C) into a 25 °C teapot. Teapot heat capacity = 230 J °C⁻¹.

Key Visual Concept: Column Width = Heat Capacity (C)
• Water Column: Wider base (Cw = 840 J °C⁻¹ → 8 blocks wide).
• Teapot Column: Narrower base (Cpot = 230 J °C⁻¹ → 3 blocks wide).
• Conservation: 8 blocks wide × 3 rows dropped = 24 blocks lost (red blocks disappear). 3 blocks wide × 8 rows raised = 24 blocks gained (orange blocks appear). Both levels settle at T = 83.9 °C!

Conservation of Energy Derivation

Heat Capacity of Water (Cw): 840 J °C⁻¹ (Wide Column)
Heat Capacity of Teapot (Cpot): 230 J °C⁻¹ (Narrow Column)
Heat Lost = Heat Gained: 840 · (100 - T) = 230 · (T - 25)
Total Energy Transferred (ΔQ): 13,542 J
Equilibrium Final Temp (Teq): 83.9 °C
Step-by-step Solution:
84,000 - 840 T = 230 T - 5,750
84,000 + 5,750 = (840 + 230) T
89,750 = 1,070 T
T = 89,750 / 1,070 = 83.88 °C ≈ 83.9 °C

Module 3: Finding Unknown Specific Heat Capacity

Problem: Alice heats a 25 g metal cube in boiling water (100 °C), then transfers it into 0.2 kg of 25 °C water. The highest recorded temperature is Tf = 27 °C.

Experimental Data

Final Measured Temp (Tf): 27.0 °C

Mathematical Deduction

Energy Gained by Water = mw · cw · (Tf - 25 °C)
Energy Lost by Metal = mm · cm · (100 °C - Tf)
ΔT of Water (Tf - 25 °C): +2.0 °C
Energy Gained by Water (Q): 1,680.0 J
ΔT of Metal (100 °C - Tf): -73.0 °C
Calculated c of Metal: 920.5 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (Aluminium)

Module 4: Immersion Heater with Real-World Heat Loss

Reference Problem: An electric immersion heater delivers power to water. In reality, some energy is lost to the surroundings.

Thermodynamic Energy Conservation:
Esupplied (P · t) = Eabsorbed (m · c · ΔT) + Elost
Useful energy absorbed cannot exceed electrical energy supplied.

Heater Parameters

Heater Power (P): 60 W
Heating Time (t): 200 s
Water Mass (m): 1.0 kg
Observed Temp Rise (ΔT): 2.0 °C

Energy Accounting Breakdown

Total Electrical Energy Supplied (P × t): 12,000 J
Useful Energy Absorbed (m · c · ΔT): 8,400 J
Energy Lost to Surroundings (Elost): 3,600 J
Thermal Efficiency (η): 70.0%

Structured Writing Guide: Heat Capacity

Use these vocabulary banks and sentence frames to construct well-explained physics explanations.
Q1 High Specific Heat Capacity of Water

Determine if each case can be explained by the high specific heat capacity of water (c), and justify your choice.

Helpful Vocabulary: specific heat capacity (c), temperature change (ΔT), internal energy (E), thermal equilibrium, polar solvent.

Writing Frames:

  • (a) Land & Sea: [Yes / No]. Water has a [much higher / lower] specific heat capacity than land/rock. For the same rate of heat loss at night, the temperature drop (ΔT) of the sea is [smaller / larger] than that of the land.
  • (b) Fried Noodles vs. Soup Noodles: [Yes / No]. Soup consists mainly of [water], which has a very high [specific heat capacity]. It stores [more / less] energy and cools down much more [slowly / rapidly] than fried noodles.
  • (c) Water as a Solvent: [Yes / No]. This is due to water’s chemical [polarity / molecular structure], which is [unrelated / related] to its thermal properties.
  • (d) Apple Pie Filling: [Yes / No]. The pie filling has a high [water content], so it has a much higher heat capacity than the dry crust, storing [more / less] internal energy at the same temperature.
Click to check answers
  • (a) Yes: Water's high specific heat capacity means it cools down more slowly than land for the same amount of heat lost.
  • (b) Yes: The soup is mostly water with high specific heat capacity, retaining thermal energy much longer than dry noodles.
  • (c) No: Water's solvent ability comes from its molecular polarity, not its thermal capacity.
  • (d) Yes: The moist filling contains abundant water, giving it a much higher heat capacity and storing far more internal energy than the crust.
Q2 Wet Sand vs. Dry Sand

When walking along the beach on a sunny day, we find the wet sand cooler than the dry sand. Why?

Helpful Vocabulary: solar radiation, specific heat capacity (c), temperature rise (ΔT = Q / mc), evaporation, latent heat of vaporization (lv).

Writing Frame:

  1. Wet sand contains water, which has a much [higher / lower] specific heat capacity than dry sand. For the same amount of solar radiation absorbed, wet sand has a [smaller / larger] temperature rise (ΔT).
  2. In addition, water continually [evaporates] from the surface, absorbing [latent heat of vaporization] and providing an extra cooling effect.
Click to view model answer
Model Answer:
1. Higher specific heat capacity: Wet sand contains water, which has a much higher specific heat capacity (c) than dry sand. By ΔT = Q / mc, the temperature rise of wet sand is smaller for the same heat absorbed.
2. Evaporative cooling: Water in wet sand evaporates, absorbing latent heat of vaporization from the sand and lowering its temperature.
Q3 Hot Spoon vs. Hot Tea

Charles puts a metal spoon in hot tea. They reach the same temperature. Why does the tea feel much hotter in the mouth than the spoon?

Helpful Vocabulary: thermal equilibrium, temperature (T), heat capacity (C = mc), internal energy transfer (ΔQ).

Writing Frame:

  • Although both are at the same [temperature], they have very different [heat capacities (C = mc)].
  • The tea (mostly water) has a much higher [specific heat capacity] and mass than the metal spoon.
  • When touching the mouth, the tea transfers a significantly [larger / smaller] amount of internal energy to the tongue before its temperature drops, causing a stronger burning sensation.
Click to view model answer
Model Answer:
Even though both are at the same temperature, the tea (primarily water) has a far higher specific heat capacity and mass than the metal spoon, meaning its total heat capacity (C = mc) is much greater. When coming into contact with the mouth, the tea transfers a much larger amount of thermal energy before cooling down, raising the temperature of the mouth tissues significantly more.
Q4 Cooling of Viscous Congee

Why does hot viscous congee cool down very slowly? Give two reasons.

Helpful Vocabulary: specific heat capacity (c), viscosity, convection currents, poor thermal conduction, surface film / evaporation.

Writing Frame:

Reason 1: Congee consists mainly of [water], which has a very high [specific heat capacity], meaning it stores a large amount of internal energy and releases it slowly.

Reason 2: Congee is very viscous (thick), which suppresses internal [convection] currents. Heat can only transfer to the surface by slow [conduction]. (Or: A starchy layer forms on top, reducing cooling by [evaporation].)

Click to view model answer
Model Answer (Any two reasons):
  1. High specific heat capacity: Congee contains a high percentage of water, which has a high specific heat capacity (c), so a large amount of energy must be lost for the temperature to fall.
  2. Suppressed convection: Because of its high viscosity, convection currents cannot readily form. Heat is transferred through the liquid primarily by slow conduction.
  3. Reduced evaporation: A starchy skin quickly forms on the surface of the congee, blocking heat loss via evaporation.

Physics Note — Temperatures are Theoretical Approximations: Calculations assume ideal conditions using standard constant specific heat values at room temperature. In reality, actual temperature rises vary because specific heat shifts slightly with temperature, heat is lost to ambient surroundings, and phase changes (melting/boiling) are not modeled.

Governing Equation: ΔT = Q m · c

Each click injects 4,184 Joules of heat into each substance:

  • 1 kg Water: ΔT = 4184 J 1 kg × 4184 J/(kg·°C) ≈ +1.00°C
  • 1 kg Iron: ΔT = 4184 J 1 kg × 450 J/(kg·°C) ≈ +9.30°C (Iron's container base is ~9.3× narrower, so the liquid level shoots up ~9.3× faster!)
  • 2 kg Water: ΔT = 4184 J 2 kg × 4184 J/(kg·°C) ≈ +0.50°C (Double the mass means double the total heat capacity C = mc, doubling the width).

Tower Height Rule: ΔHeight = Total Bricks Added (Q) Base Footprint (C)

Because Water stores ~9.3 times more heat than Iron per kilogram, a single layer of temperature rise requires a foundation that is 9 bricks wide, while Iron needs only 1 brick.

  • Adding 9 bricks to Iron adds 9 vertical layers (≈ +9.3°C rise).
  • Adding 9 bricks to 1 kg Water completes just 1 layer (≈ +1.0°C rise).
  • Adding 9 bricks to 2 kg Water completes only half a layer (≈ +0.5°C rise).

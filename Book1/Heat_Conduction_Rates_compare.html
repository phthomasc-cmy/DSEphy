Why do conduction rates differ so much?

• Solid: Because atoms are locked tightly in a lattice structure, when one atom heats up and vibrates, it immediately knocks against its adjacent neighbors, propagating thermal energy rapidly across the material.

• Liquid: Particles are close together, but because they flow and lack fixed geometry, energy transfer via molecular collisions is somewhat disrupted and slower.

• Gas: Particles are widely separated by large empty spaces. A heated particle has to travel a long distance before hitting another particle, making gases extremely poor heat conductors (effectively thermal insulators).

(a) Find the heat capacity of the pot of soup

Step 1: What is the temperature change (ΔT) of the soup?

Step 2: Calculate the Heat Capacity (C)

Recall: Q = C × ΔT  ⇒  C = QΔT

View Full Working for Part (a)

1. Temperature change (ΔT):
ΔT = Final Temperature − Initial Temperature
ΔT = 80 °C − 25 °C = 55 °C (or 55 K)


2. Energy supplied (Q):
Q = 44 kJ = 44,000 J


3. Heat Capacity (C):

C = QΔT = 44 kJ55 °C = 0.8 kJ / °C (or 800 J / °C)

(b) Doubled Heat Capacity

More water is added so that the heat capacity of the soup is doubled. Find the temperature when energy of 44 kJ is supplied.

Step 1: What is the new temperature rise (ΔT′)?

Think: If heat capacity doubles, what happens to the temperature rise for the same amount of heat?

°C
Step 2: What is the final temperature?

Remember to add the temperature rise to the initial temperature (25 °C).

°C
View Full Working for Part (b)

1. New Heat Capacity (C′):
C′ = 2 × 0.8 kJ/°C = 1.6 kJ/°C (or 1600 J/°C)


2. New temperature change (ΔT′):

ΔT′ = QC′ = 44 kJ1.6 kJ/°C = 27.5 °C

(Notice: doubling C halves the temperature rise from 55 °C to 27.5 °C)


3. Final temperature:
Final Temperature = Initial Temperature + ΔT′
= 25 °C + 27.5 °C = 52.5 °C

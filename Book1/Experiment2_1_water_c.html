Experimental Precautions & Scientific Reasons

SAFETY & ACCURACY 1. Completely immerse the heating part before switching on
Scientific Reason: Air is an extremely poor thermal conductor compared to water. If the heating coil operates partially in air, it cannot dissipate heat quickly and will rapidly overheat, potentially burning out the coil. Furthermore, heat transferred directly to room air bypasses the water, causing an overestimation of electrical energy supplied to the water ($\Delta E$).
ACCURACY 2. Cover the polystyrene cup with a lid
Scientific Reason: Warm water readily evaporates and induces natural air convection above the surface. Water has a very large latent heat of vaporisation ($\approx 2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J kg}^{-1}$); even a tiny amount of evaporation carries away substantial thermal energy, causing the recorded temperature rise ($\Delta T$) to be lower and producing an artificially high calculated value for $c$.
ACCURACY 3. Keep stirring the water gently throughout
Scientific Reason: Water is a poor thermal conductor. Without stirring, localized hot pockets form close to the heating element while outer water remains cooler. Gentle stirring guarantees a uniform bulk temperature, ensuring the thermometer accurately records the true bulk average temperature. (Note: Stir gently to avoid splashing water out or introducing mechanical friction heating.)
ACCURACY 4. Place the cup on an insulating tile (if necessary)
Scientific Reason: Bench surfaces (especially slate or metal lab tables) are large heat sinks. Placing the cup on an insulating tile minimises conductive heat loss through the bottom of the container to the benchtop.
SAFETY & ACCURACY 5. Ensure the heater does not touch the cup wall or thermometer bulb
Scientific Reason: The surface of the heating element reaches temperatures far higher than boiling water. Direct contact with the polystyrene wall will melt or puncture the cup, causing water leaks. Direct contact with the thermometer bulb will record the heater coil's surface temperature rather than the water's temperature, and can crack the glass bulb due to localized thermal shock.

Discussion Questions & Standard Solutions

1. What is the advantage of using a polystyrene cup as the container? +
Polystyrene is an excellent thermal insulator and has a very low heat capacity. This minimises unwanted heat loss to the surrounding air and ensures negligible thermal energy is absorbed by the cup itself.
2. Why should the water be stirred throughout the process? +
Water is a poor conductor of heat. Stirring promotes convection and ensures a uniform temperature distribution across the whole volume, avoiding hot spots near the heater and giving an accurate bulk temperature.
3. Why is the experimental value often larger than the standard value ($4200\text{ J kg}^{-1}{^\circ\text{C}}^{-1}$)? +
From the formula $c = \dfrac{\Delta E}{m\,\Delta T}$:
  • (a) The cup and stirrer also warm up: Some measured energy $\Delta E$ is absorbed by the container, stirrer, and thermometer, meaning $\Delta T$ of the water is lower than expected $\rightarrow$ calculated $c$ becomes larger.
  • (b) Heat loss to the surroundings: Dissipated heat lowers the final temperature rise $\Delta T$ for the supplied electrical energy $\rightarrow$ calculated $c$ becomes larger.
  • (c) The mass of water is overestimated: Since $m$ is in the denominator, overestimating $m$ would yield a smaller $c$, so this is not a reason for an overestimate! (You can verify this in the Error Studio tab).
4. Why does the water temperature rise even though the heater is switched off? +
The heating coil has internal heat capacity and becomes significantly hotter than the surrounding water. When switched off, residual heat continues conducting from the coil into the cooler water until thermal equilibrium is established.

Why record the highest temperature? Because all electrical energy registered by the joulemeter entered the heater coil. Using the peak temperature ($T_1$) ensures that all supplied energy transferred into the water is properly accounted for.
